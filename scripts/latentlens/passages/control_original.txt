On the second Tuesday of March the reading room on Alder Street stayed open past nine, because the heating in the town hall had failed again and the council needed somewhere warm to argue. Mrs. Okafor, who had run the room for eleven years, set out the folding chairs in two crooked rows and put the kettle on before anyone asked. The question before them was small and stubborn: whether the old map drawers by the window should be sold to pay for a new roof, or kept, with the roof patched for one more winter. Nobody disputed that the drawers were beautiful. Several people disputed that beauty was a budget line. By ten o'clock the vote was tied, the tea was cold, and the youngest member suggested, to general relief, that they meet again when the hall was warm.
